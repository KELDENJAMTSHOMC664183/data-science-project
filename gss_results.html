How to read this (honest summary): within any given year the income–happiness gradient is large, significant by every metric (χ²(8) = 2861.3, p < 1e-300; OR = 2.52, CI [2.39, 2.66]; Cohen's d = 0.59, CI [0.56, 0.61]) and survives age/sex/education controls. Across time the two arms diverge: real GDP per capita grew ×2.49 while the share saying "very happy" fell 30.3% → 19.8%, and using all 35 survey waves the pre-2022 trend is significantly downward (Kendall τ = −0.47, p = 0.0001) — a richer America that became measurably less happy, not merely flat. That decline is already present in the face-to-face era alone (1972–2018: τ = −0.44, p = 0.0003), so it is not an artefact of the 2021–24 collection-mode change or the pandemic; those waves merely steepen the drop. Caveats: (1) income is published in coarse bands (12–26 midpoints per wave), so we rank within year instead of trusting cross-era dollar values; (2) cross-sectional association ≠ causation (health, marriage and personality correlate with both); (3) survey happiness is self-reported. Bottom line: money buys happiness relative to your fellow Americans; nationally, the 2.5× rise in income bought none.
